Season narrative — after 13 rounds · Italy GP
Antonelli leads the championship by 66 points over Russell and 75 over Hamilton with 10 races remaining — the title is firmly in his hands but not yet mathematically decided. His Monza victory from 19th on the grid was one of the drives of the decade: the first Italian to win his home race since 1966, carving through the field after a back-of-grid power unit penalty and overtaking Russell in the closing laps. Russell is now P2 in the championship (221 pts), having jumped Hamilton after a strong Monza P2. Ferrari had a disastrous Italy — Leclerc crashed on lap 3 after a Hamilton clash at the chicane; Ferrari scored zero constructors' points. McLaren are closing on Ferrari in the constructors (289 vs 338) after Piastri P4 and Norris P5 at Monza. Red Bull's Verstappen took P3 — back-to-back podiums confirm the upgrade is real. Mercedes admitted no budget for further 2026 upgrades — a seismic revelation that shifts the development narrative for the rest of the season. The Monaco Gasly podium appeal (McLaren & Red Bull vs Alpine) remains pending. Next: Madrid GP · Ifema Circuit · Sep 11–13 — F1's street circuit debut in the Spanish capital.

The 2026 regulations are a complete reset. Every 2025 data point is obsolete.

Active Aerodynamics — replaces DRS
Moveable wings open on straights ("Straight Mode") to cut drag, close in corners ("Corner Mode") for downforce. Every driver, every lap. Straight-line speed and cornering are no longer a simple trade-off.
50/50 Power Split — Electric vs Combustion
Equal power from ICE and MGU-K (~550 kW each). Battery depletes fast — cars harvest energy for stretches each lap. Energy management software is now as decisive as hardware. Russell's Canada retirement was a direct PU failure.
Overtake Mode — replaces DRS for racing
Within 1s of the car ahead: up to 350 kW extra electrical deployment, works in corners too — overtaking now develops across sequences, not just straights.
New Power Unit Manufacturers
RB Powertrains/Ford — Red Bull & Racing Bulls. Audi — works team. Honda — exclusive to Aston Martin. Alpine — switched to Mercedes customer. McLaren, Haas, Cadillac on Mercedes or Ferrari units.
Cadillac — The 11th Team

Additional Development and Upgrade Opportunities — the 2026 engine catch-up mechanism
What is ADUO?
ADUO allows underperforming power unit manufacturers to make additional upgrades to homologated engines mid-season. The FIA monitors each manufacturer's ICE Performance Index — based on engine torque, engine speed, MGU-K power, and lap-time sensitivity to power output — and compares it against the benchmark (fastest) engine. Any manufacturer 2% or more behind the benchmark qualifies. It is not a Balance of Performance system — it only targets ICE power gaps, not championship standings. Red Bull are 6th in the constructors but their engine is competitive, so they get nothing; Aston Martin are last but their Honda PU is far off the pace, so they get maximum relief.

Key Rules & Politics
ADUO is granted only once per review period — not each race. An unused upgrade allocation expires at season end.

The gamesmanship angle: Toto Wolff argued ADUO should not become a "leapfrog mechanism" and that only Honda truly needs it. Charles Leclerc publicly claimed Ferrari is even behind Red Bull/Ford in pure ICE power. The FIA has noted these public statements play no role — its decision is data-only.

Why Red Bull gets nothing despite being 6th: Their RB22 chassis is the problem, not the engine. ADUO measures ICE power only. Red Bull Powertrains is reportedly the second-best engine on the grid — their pace deficit is entirely aerodynamic and mechanical.
